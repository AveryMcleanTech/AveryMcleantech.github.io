The Experiment: Is Race "Real"?

This biotech and social science experiment investigated whether the concept of human race has a basis in biology (genetics) versus psychology and sociology. The experiment utilized data from the Implicit Association Test (IAT), a psychological research tool designed to uncover subconscious biases that individuals might be unaware of or unwilling to admit.

During the test, participants rapidly categorize words or images (such as faces of different racial backgrounds) alongside positive or negative evaluations (like "good" or "bad") on a computer screen. By measuring the exact speed of these responses down to the millisecond, the test determines the strength of a person's automatic mental associations.

Core Finding: The data reveals that while humans share 99% of their DNA—proving that discrete biological racial boundaries do not exist in genetics—race is intensely "real" from a psychological and social standpoint. Because society collectively agrees to act as if these categories are true, they create measurable implicit biases that directly alter human behavior and social structures.

Project Reflection

What I Did and Learned

In this project, I analyzed the intersection of genetics and social psychology to answer whether race is biologically or socially real. I learned that from a strictly biotechnological perspective, race is a human invention; genetic variation is continuous and overlaps entirely across populations rather than stopping at arbitrary racial lines. However, by studying the mechanics and typical data of the Implicit Association Test (IAT), I discovered how deeply embedded the construct of race is within the human psyche. The experiment taught me that social constructs do not need a biological foundation to have massive real-world power. Because people collectively internalize cultural messaging, these categories manifest as subconscious implicit biases that shape how individuals perceive, judge, and interact with one another daily.

21st Century Skill & Growth

This experiment allowed me to demonstrate significant growth in the 21st-century skill of Critical Thinking and Evidence-Based Reasoning. To complete this analysis, I had to look past surface-level societal assumptions and evaluate raw scientific data objectively. Synthesizing two vastly different fields of study—genomic data showing 99% shared human DNA alongside cognitive processing data from the IAT—forced me to reconcile scientific facts with complex societal frameworks. I showed mastery by constructing a tight, logical Claim-Evidence-Reasoning argument, ensuring that my final conclusions were driven entirely by experimental evidence rather than personal bias or casual observation.
